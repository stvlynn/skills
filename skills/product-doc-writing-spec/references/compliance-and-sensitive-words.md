# Legal Compliance and Sensitive Wording Guidelines

This reference preserves the compliance rules for avoiding false advertising, absolute superlatives, and improper citations in technical and product documentation.

---

## 1. Superlatives and Advertising Law Compliance

### 1.1 Prohibited High-Risk Absolute Claims
Product documentation and official pages must present objective, verifiable facts. Unless backed by statutory exceptions, **never use absolute superlative claims**, including:
- **"National-level" / "State-level"**
- **Any unqualified superlative ("most" / "-est")**: e.g., *highest-level*, *best*, *most professional*, *most secure*, *most popular*.
- **Explicitly banned absolute promotional terms**: *top-tier / supreme*, *No. 1 brand*, *peerless*, *sovereign*, *ultimate / extreme*.

### 1.2 Permitted Exceptions (Only When Verifiable Proof Exists)
Absolute or comparative terms may be used **only** when supported by valid, archived legal or technical evidence under the following five specific conditions:
1. **Objective Factual Statements**:
   - Examples: *"1st Place in [Official Competition Name]"*, or rankings published by an authoritative third-party research institution.
   - Requirement: Retain the award certificate or official report, and clearly state the **award name, issuing body, date/validity period, and scope limitations** in the text.
2. **Chronological / First-Release Statements**:
   - Examples: *"first-of-its-kind"*, *"first release"*, *"earliest"*, *"exclusive"*, *"only"*, such as *"our first serverless database service"*.
   - Requirement: Must be backed by valid copyright registrations, release records, or exclusive authorization agreements.
3. **Explicit Self-Comparison Across Internal Tiers**:
   - Examples: Objective comparisons among a product's own tiers or historical offerings (e.g., *"our highest-throughput instance family"* or *"our most popular plan"*), provided it is explicitly framed as an internal comparison and does not contradict other published rules.
4. **Recognized Industry-Standard Grading**:
   - Where an official national or industry standard defines tier names that contain superlative words, those standard tier names may be used if accompanied by the grading standard citation and inspection certificate.
5. **Explicit Corporate Vision / Aspirational Goals Without Misleading Users**:
   - Examples: *"Customer First, Pursuing Perfect Quality"*.
   - Requirement: Must clearly read as an organizational philosophy rather than an objective guarantee that the product itself is "perfect" or "#1".

### 1.3 Data and Citation Compliance
When citing statistics, benchmark data, survey results, excerpts, or quotes in documentation:
1. All cited data must be **authentic, accurate, and attributed to its original source**. If the data has a specific scope or validity period, state it explicitly.
2. Citations must **not** contain false claims or prohibited superlatives.
3. Citations must faithfully reflect the original source without fabrication, cherry-picking, distortion, or misleading omission.

### 1.4 Objective Product Descriptions
- Ensure all product descriptions (such as SLA commitments, performance metrics, and billing/promotional rules) match actual system behavior with rigorous logic.
- **Never make unverified promises** regarding suitability or business outcomes, and **never disparage competitors**.
